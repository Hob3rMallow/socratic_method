\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}

We calibrate thresholds from 0.25 through 0.60 in increments of 0.01 on
registered PHerc0814 and PHerc1451 patches.
Checkpoint ranking uses calibrated macro-scroll Dice with a minimum per-scroll
gain guard against released M7.
Promotion additionally requires a blinded PHerc1447 anti-blob review, two-sided
morphology and coverage checks, and postprocessor false-join review.
An earlier raw v29 candidate failed the PHerc1447 gate; we do not consider it a
viable model.

\subsection{Duration Study}

Table~\ref{tab:duration} reports every committed validation interval through the
completed \observedSamplesText-sample schedule.
All intervals improve both held-out scrolls over released M7.
The response is shallow and non-monotonic; the best ordinary validation result
is the \bestSamplesText-sample interval at $\bestMacro$ calibrated macro-scroll
Dice. This behavior justifies retaining the full ladder and separating
validation ranking from the morphology-based release decision.

The trust-region projection is active for 97.7\% of optimizer updates in the
first interval and 100\% thereafter.
The method therefore reaches its best observed validation score while remaining
on the boundary of its allowed parameter neighborhood.

Every interval selects threshold 0.25, the lower edge of our ordinary Dice
sweep. The optimum is censored, and the corresponding predictions inflate
foreground on blind PHerc1447; 0.25 is therefore rejected. Independent
registered morphology and anti-blob review selects the raw
\releaseSamplesText-sample student at $t=\releaseThreshold$.

At the selected point, the six PHerc1447 cubes contain 0.951 times the
foreground of the v15 anti-blob comparison and retain 0.911 of that comparison's
foreground. All aggregate and per-cube anti-blob constraints pass, with no
interior-fraction or maximum-thickness regressions. On the 16 locked PHerc0139
slices, all anti-blob checks pass, mean ASSD is 0.626 voxels, and 15 component
counts match. Rank 26 is the exception: the teacher truly has four components,
while two top blobs and a touching pair of strokes yield five components in the
student despite recovering the intended structures.

FLIP mean, which rewards literal teacher resemblance, narrowly prefers 4,096
samples at $t=0.42$ (0.22836 versus 0.23331 for the release). This is not hidden:
the selected point ranks 18 of 45 anti-blob-eligible settings by that statistic.
Against the 2,048-sample checkpoint at fixed $t=0.45$, however, 8,192 improves
FLIP on 12 of 16 slices while reducing both teacher-missing pixels
(4,283 to 4,270) and additions (1,201 to 1,155). Human review and blind de-blob
behavior resolve the remaining choice in favor of the longer run.

\subsection{Line-Fitter Evaluation}

In a calibrated $4\!\times\!5\!\times\!5$ study, the line fitter accepted 481
joins and added 12,697 pixels.
Registered high-resolution precision was 92.5\% overall and 98.4\% for joins no
longer than six pixels.
We observed no full-turn fusion and measured a 21\% reduction in atlas overlap.
A larger 21-cube census measured 98.3\% weighted precision.

Adjacent high-resolution connectivity is useful confirmation but is not, by
itself, proof that a join remains on one wrap.
The umbilicus-aware radial gate is our primary cross-wrap safeguard.
The release evaluation will therefore report false joins by gate, distance, and
radial regime rather than only an aggregate precision.

\subsection{Interpretation}

The duration experiment establishes consistent positive ordinary held-out gains
under a saturated M7 trust constraint. The independent gates establish a
selected raw checkpoint and operating threshold, but not ground-truth accuracy:
the PHerc0139 teacher and PHerc1447 v15 reference are both model-derived. The
release record therefore preserves metric exceptions and human-review decisions
alongside aggregate scores rather than collapsing them into one pass bit.
