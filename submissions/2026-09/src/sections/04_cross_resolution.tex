\section{Cross-Resolution Supervision}
\label{sec:crossres}

\subsection{Student Architecture and Data}

Our student is a six-stage 3D residual-encoder nnU-Net with feature counts
$(32,64,128,256,320,320)$, one CT input channel, and two output classes.
It has $102{,}349{,}770$ trainable parameters.
Every parameter is retrained, but initialization is accepted only when the
released M7 checkpoint matches its recorded SHA-256; the check is fail-closed
and includes the original segmentation heads.

The training corpus contains \corpusRows\ registered $192^3$ rows:
\corpusTrain\ training and \corpusVal\ validation, drawn from \corpusScrolls\
training scrolls and two held-out validation scrolls.
Of the training rows, \corpusNative\ carry anti-aliased soft teacher targets
and \corpusHuman\ are human-labelled rows passed through as hard targets.
We draw \fzHorizon\ sample exposures from this schedule; ``\fzHorizon'' counts
repeated draws, not unique rows.

Corpus breadth is the single largest lever we measured, and it is worth stating
plainly because it displaced most of our loss engineering.
An earlier configuration of this method trained on 4,096 rows from one scroll.
Widening to \corpusScrolls\ scrolls, with no other change to the objective,
moved the frozen benchmark further than any auxiliary loss term we designed
(Appendix~\ref{sec:also-tried}).

Our held-out validation manifest contains registered PHerc0814 and PHerc1451
patches, frozen before this study. We evaluate every $10{,}000$ exposures and
retain a model-only snapshot at each of the 25 milestones. Because the
milestone response is a shallow random walk rather than a monotone curve
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results}), we neither assume the final checkpoint is best nor
select on the best single milestone without accounting for seed noise.

\subsection{Fine-Teacher Occupancy}

The fine teacher provides one target in the student's physical frame: soft
occupancy $q\in[0,1]$, obtained by an anti-aliased pullback from the
$2.399\,\mu$m grid, with an independent validity mask $V$ recording where the
teacher is defined at all.
Occupancy estimates how much of a $9.362\,\mu$m voxel is filled by papyrus, and
retaining it as a fraction rather than a hard vote preserves the partial-volume
evidence at a sheet boundary that motivated the cross-resolution setup.

\paragraph{The occupancy ceiling, and why it is a corpus property.}
A thin but real continuation commonly occupies only 25--45\% of a coarse voxel.
Soft cross-entropy is correctly calibrated when it drives such a voxel toward
$p=0.25$--$0.45$, and the result may then sit below the segmentation threshold.
Read as a property of the loss, this observation says that occupancy supervision
has a ceiling and needs a separate sheet-existence target; that reading produced
the medial crest term described in Appendix~\ref{sec:also-tried}.
Read as a property of the data, it says something narrower: on a single-scroll
schedule, the fraction of continuation-band voxels the student can push over a
usable threshold is small.
The second reading is the correct one.
On the present corpus the same diagnostic does not fire---a clear majority of
continuation-band validation voxels already sit above the lowest gate-passing
threshold---and the crest term, measured there, does not help.
We retain occupancy alone.

\subsection{Objective}

Let $p$ be the student's surface probability, $q$ the projected fine-teacher
occupancy, $V$ its validity field, and $p_0$ the released M7 probability from a
frozen copy of the initializer carried through training.
Our loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(p,q)
 + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Dice}}(p,q)
 + 0.5\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}}(p,p_0).
\label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
All terms are evaluated only where their validity fields permit.
Every other term we have implemented carries weight zero, and there is no
parameter-space projection; Appendices~\ref{sec:also-tried}
and~\ref{sec:trust} record what those terms were and how they were measured.

\paragraph{Soft occupancy.}
Cross-entropy and Dice both take the fractional target $q$ rather than a
thresholded label, and both are reduced per sample before averaging over the
samples that contain any supervised voxel.
The per-sample reduction is deliberate: a dense human-labelled patch and a
sparse native-teacher patch otherwise contribute in proportion to their valid
voxel counts, which lets one supervision source dominate a mixed corpus.

\paragraph{M7 function anchor.}
The teacher is locally more informative than M7, not globally complete, and
across most of any training volume it says nothing at all.
We therefore anchor the student to the released function wherever the teacher
is silent or confidently agrees, and leave it free everywhere else.
Let $H=\{q\geq0.5\}\cap V$ be the teacher's hard positives,
$h_x=\mathbf{1}[x\in H]$, $m_x=\mathbf{1}[p_{0,x}\geq0.5]$, and let $D_2$
denote radius-two dilation. The anchor mask is
\begin{align}
 K={}&\bigl(\overline{V}\setminus D_2(H)\bigr) \nonumber\\
 &{}\cup\{x\in V:h_x=m_x\ \wedge\ (q_x\leq0.1\ \vee\ h_x=1)\},
 \label{eq:kl-mask}
\end{align}
and the term is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}}
 =\frac{1}{|K|}\sum_{x\in K}
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\operatorname{Ber}(p_{0,x})\,
 \middle\|\,\operatorname{Ber}(p_x)\right).
 \label{eq:m7-kl}
\end{equation}

Three properties of $K$ carry the term's behaviour, and all three were needed
before it measured positive.
First, unknown space is anchored, so the student cannot invent structure where
nothing has been observed.
Second, a radius-two corridor around known teacher positives is carved back out
of that unknown region, so M7 is not permitted to freeze the immediate
neighbourhood of a sheet the teacher wants grown.
Third, inside known space the anchor acts only where M7 and the teacher agree
\emph{and} the teacher is confident, meaning $q\leq0.1$ or $q\geq0.5$.
Without this last restriction, a partial-volume voxel at $q=0.3$ with M7 below
its own decision boundary counts as agreement on background, and the anchor
then pulls down exactly the continuation band the occupancy target is trying to
raise.
The direction $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathrm{Ber}(p_0)\|\mathrm{Ber}(p))$ is
mode-covering with respect to M7: it penalizes the student for abandoning M7's
mass, not for adding its own where M7 is unopposed.

\paragraph{Deep supervision.}
Both occupancy terms and the anchor are applied at the standard nnU-Net decoder
outputs with normalized weights proportional to $1,1/2,1/4,\ldots$ and zero
weight on the coarsest output. Soft occupancy is resized trilinearly and its
validity is true only where every contributing fine cell is valid.

\subsection{Optimization}

We use SGD with learning rate $10^{-3}$, Nesterov momentum $0.99$, weight decay
$3\times10^{-5}$, gradient clipping at $12$, and a polynomial decay of exponent
$0.9$ declared over the full \fzHorizon-exposure horizon.
The batch size is three with no gradient accumulation, under bfloat16 autocast.
Seed 1203 fixes the sample order and augmentation sequence.

Declaring the schedule at its true length matters for comparability.
The learning rate at exposure $n$ depends on the declared horizon, so a run
stopped early is a different optimizer trajectory from a run that was declared
short, and milestone scores taken while the rate is still near its initial value
describe a hot random walk rather than a converged model.
Every arm in this study, including the ablations of
Appendix~\ref{sec:also-tried}, declares the same \fzHorizon\ horizon so that
matched milestones are comparable even when an arm is terminated early.

There is no trust region and no post-update projection.
Earlier versions of this method projected the parameter vector into a
relative-$L^2$ ball around released M7 after every update.
That constraint, its saturation, and the measurement it invalidated are the
subject of Appendix~\ref{sec:trust}.
